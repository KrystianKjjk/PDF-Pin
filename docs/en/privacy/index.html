1. In short

PDF Pin is an iPhone app for annotating plans and drawings in PDF format: you drop a pin on a page, take a photo, and the app appends it as a new page of the same file together with the location, time and a note.

  • Your data is stored on your device. The app has no servers of its own and requires no account. We never receive your documents, photos, location or notes.
  • We collect no statistics and do not track users. The app contains no analytics, advertising or third-party libraries of any kind.
  • Your files leave the device only through Google Drive — an optional sync that you turn on yourself — when you share them yourself, and in the standard iOS backup if you have it enabled (section 6). The sync transfers copies of your PDFs and photos solely between the app and your own Google Drive.
  • To turn GPS into an address and to dictate notes, the app uses Apple services, which may process the coordinates and the voice recording on Apple’s servers — see section 6.

Aggregated statistics from Apple (section 6) do not allow us to identify you and are not personal data.

3. Principle: the app works only on your device

Everything you create in PDF Pin is stored in the app’s private folder on the device (the “PDF Pin” folder visible in the Files app) and in the app’s local database. The app never connects to any server of ours, because there is no such server.

Network connections are made in only three situations: (1) when you enable Google Drive sync — to Google’s servers (sign-in, uploading, renaming and deleting files); (2) when the app converts GPS coordinates into an address — to Apple’s geocoding service; (3) when you dictate a note — to Apple’s speech recognition service, if the system cannot process it on the device. None of these connections send anything to us.

4. What data the app processes and why

Below we list every kind of data the app works with. All of it is processed locally and solely so that the app can do its job. You decide which of it is printed on the photo page in the PDF with the “PDF page content” settings (globally or for a single project); regardless of those settings, the data is stored in the app and shown in the pin view.

  • PDF documents — Files you import (from the Files app, via “Share” or “Open in”). The app makes a copy of the file in the project folder and works on that copy — the original is left untouched. Pins (annotations) and photo pages are written into the copy. The sample project (“See an example”) is generated by the app and contains none of your data.
  • Photos — Photos taken with the in-app camera or picked from your library. Each photo is saved as a JPEG file in the project’s “photos” subfolder (at full resolution) and inserted as a new PDF page (at a resolution reduced to max 1920 px). The photo is re-encoded on save, so it does not the saved file does not contain the camera’s original metadata (EXIF). From a photo picked from your library the app reads the date and place it was taken stored in its metadata (if present) and uses them as the pin’s capture time and location — instead of the current time and the phone’s current position. It neither reads nor stores any other metadata. A small thumbnail is also kept for the pin list.
  • Location (GPS) — Latitude, longitude, altitude and measurement accuracy: for a camera photo — the phone’s position when the photo is taken; for a library photo — only the place stored in the photo’s metadata (without it the pin has no location, and you can type the address yourself). Stored in the app database with the pin; if the “Location” option is enabled, the coordinates and their accuracy are also printed on the photo page in the PDF. The app reads the phone’s position only if you grant location permission, and only while it is open: after you take a photo, and while you view a document — in the latter case solely to show the GPS signal accuracy in the header (refreshed every minute); that position is not stored anywhere.
  • Address — An approximate address (town, street, number) derived from the GPS coordinates using Apple’s geocoding service (see section 6). You can correct it or type it in before saving. Stored together with the location; printed on the photo page when the “Location” option is enabled.
  • Notes and names — Text you type or dictate for a pin, plus project and document names. The note is printed on the photo page in the PDF if the “Note” option is enabled. Project and document names are also the names of folders and files on the device (and on Google Drive when sync is on).
  • Dates and numbers — Pin creation date, photo capture date (for a library photo without a date in its metadata — the date it was added, labelled “Added”), pin number and its position on the page. Needed to navigate between a pin and its photo page. The time is printed on the photo page if the “Capture time” option is enabled.
  • Settings — Theme colour, language, PDF page content (global and per project), the “Upload over Wi-Fi only” option, whether the tutorial has been completed. Stored locally.
  • Diagnostic log — Like most iOS apps, PDF Pin writes short technical entries to the device’s system log: error codes, counters, operation times and the names of the projects and files they relate to. The system keeps them on the device and deletes them on its own after a while. A log file is created only at your request (“Settings → Diagnostics → Share diagnostic log”, or as an attachment to a bug report), covers only the current app session, and goes wherever you send it; its temporary copy is deleted from the device when you leave Settings, or at the latest on the next app launch. It never contains document contents, photos, notes, GPS coordinates, e-mail addresses or access tokens.

5. System permissions

The app asks for a permission only when it is needed. You can refuse any of them — the app keeps working, just without that feature. You can change permissions at any time in iOS Settings.

  • Camera — Taking photos pinned to places in the PDF. Without it you can pick a photo from your library.
  • Location (while using) — Saving GPS coordinates and an address with the photo, and the GPS accuracy indicator in the document view — which is why the system asks the first time you open a document. Without it pins are saved without location (library photos keep the place stored in their metadata). With “Precise Location” turned off, the app marks the position as approximate. The app never uses location in the background.
  • Microphone — Dictating notes. Without it you type notes with the keyboard.
  • Speech recognition — Turning a dictated note into text. Without it dictation is unavailable.
  • Photos (library) — Picking a photo from your library goes through Apple’s system picker, which hands the app only the photos you select — the app has no access to your whole library and does not ask for it. The picker hands over the photo together with its metadata, including where it was taken; you can turn off including the location in the picker’s options.

6. Apple services used by the app

Some features are performed by iOS on Apple’s side. In those cases Apple processes the data under Apple’s privacy policy (apple.com/legal/privacy). Apart from the reports described below, we receive no data from these services.

  • Geocoding (address from GPS) — To show an address next to a photo, the app passes the GPS coordinates to Apple’s geocoding service (CoreLocation). This happens only when a photo is taken with location enabled, or when you pick a library photo that has its place stored in its metadata.
  • Speech recognition — A dictated note is processed by Apple’s speech recognition service. Depending on the device and language, the recording may be sent to Apple’s servers to be converted into text. The app does not store recordings — it saves only the recognised text.
  • iCloud / computer backup — The app’s data — the folder with your files, the database with pin metadata (including location, addresses and notes) and the settings, including your Google account e-mail and the list of uploaded files — is included in the standard iOS backup if you have it enabled. This is a system setting independent of the app. The Google token (section 7) cannot be restored from a backup onto another device.
  • Crash reports and statistics — If you agree in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers), Apple may give us crash reports (including the device model, the system version and the state of the program at the moment of the crash) and aggregated usage statistics. Independently of that, Apple gives us aggregated download statistics. None of these contain your documents, photos, location or notes, and none allow us to identify you. You can withdraw this consent at any time in the same settings.
  • App Store — Downloading the app and any payments are handled by Apple. We receive neither your payment details nor your Apple account data.

7. Google Drive — optional sync

Google Drive sync is off until you turn it on by signing in to your Google account inside the app. It keeps copies of your projects on your own Drive. Once connected, the app uploads your existing projects (except the sample project) and then every change automatically.

  • Signing in — Takes place on Google’s page, in a system browser window. The app never sees your password — it receives only access tokens from Google.
  • What is sent — Copies of the project PDF files (with pins and photo pages) and the JPEG photo files. They go to the folder “PDF Pin/<project name>/” on your Google Drive, so project and file names become folder and file names on Drive. Location, address, capture time and note reach Drive only to the extent they are printed on the photo pages of the PDF (section 4). We do not send the local database or settings.
  • Where it is sent — Only to Google’s servers, directly from your device, over an encrypted HTTPS connection. The data passes through no intermediate server — we never see it.
  • Access scope — The app asks Google for the narrowest possible scope (“drive.file”): it can see and modify only the files it created itself. It has no access to the rest of your Drive.
  • What is stored on the device — A Google session refresh token — in the system keychain, protected as “this device only”: the token is not transferred to other devices, including when a backup is restored; the e-mail address of your Google account, shown in the app so you know which account is being synced; a list of uploaded files (name, size, modification time) so the app does not upload unchanged files again; a list of Drive deletions waiting for a connection (project and file names). We do not store your Google password.
  • Disconnecting — “Disconnect” (tap the Google Drive bar on the project list) stops the sync, removes the token, the e-mail address, the list of uploaded files and the list of pending deletions from the device, and asks Google to revoke the permission granted to the app — the app then loses access to your account and disappears from the list of apps with access to it. Files already uploaded stay on Drive. If the device is offline when you disconnect, the revocation may fail and the app does not retry it — in that case revoke access in your Google account settings (myaccount.google.com/permissions).
  • Deletion — When deleting a project or document the app asks whether to delete the Drive copy as well. Deleting a single pin in a synced project updates the PDF on Drive and moves that pin’s photo to the Drive trash. If the device is offline at that moment, the deletion from Drive waits and is carried out once the connection returns (the Google Drive bar shows this until it is done). If you disconnect the account before then, the copy stays on Drive — delete it manually at drive.google.com. Files deleted from Drive go to the Google Drive trash under Google’s rules. Every new save of a file creates a new version of it on Drive; Google may keep earlier versions in the file’s version history for a while.

Data obtained from Google Drive (including the account e-mail address) is used solely to provide the sync features visible to you in the app. We do not pass it to anyone, use it for advertising, or for any other purpose. Our use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google’s processing of your data is governed by Google’s privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy).

8. Data you share yourself

The app lets you send files out, but always on your initiative and through the system “Share” sheet. This applies to the project PDFs (with pins and photo pages), the diagnostic log and this policy. You decide who receives them and through which app — the recipient is responsible for any further processing. A shared PDF contains everything printed on its photo pages — depending on the “PDF page content” settings, also the address and GPS coordinates, the capture time and the note. The app’s folder is also visible in the Files app, where you can copy and delete files, including the JPEG photos.

Bug reports. Under Settings → Diagnostics you can send a report to pdfpinapp@icloud.com. The app prepares the message — your description, the app version, the system version, the Google Drive connection status, and the number of projects, documents and pins — and hands it to your mail app. You can also attach the diagnostic log; it includes project and file names, but not their contents: no photos, notes or locations. You see the whole message before anything is sent, and you tap “Send” yourself. The app itself makes no network connection for this, and none to any server of ours. We use reports solely to diagnose and fix bugs, and delete them once they are no longer needed.

9. What the app does not do

  • It has no user accounts and requires no registration.
  • It collects no usage statistics, crash data or device identifiers on its own — you send bug reports yourself and see their contents first (section 8), and crash reports can reach us only from Apple, if you agree to it (section 6).
  • It shows no ads and uses no ad networks.
  • It contains no third-party libraries or SDKs.
  • It does not sell, rent or share your data with anyone.
  • It does not track you across other apps or websites (Apple privacy manifest: “Tracking: no”).
  • It makes no automated decisions about you and does no profiling.
  • It is not directed at children and does not knowingly process children’s data.

10. How long data is kept

For as long as you decide. The data lives on your device, so:

  • deleting a document or project in the app removes its files (PDFs and photos) and database entries — unless you choose “Remove from app only”, which leaves the files in the app’s folder, visible in the Files app;
  • a pin cancelled while being added is removed immediately; you can also delete any pin yourself (touch and hold it on the drawing) — its photo page is removed from the PDF and the photo file deleted; pins are also removed together with their document;
  • uninstalling the app removes its entire folder, database and settings from the device;
  • copies on Google Drive stay on your Drive until you delete them — in the app (the “Delete from phone and Google Drive” option) or directly on Drive;
  • the Google token is removed when you disconnect. Uninstalling does not delete it — iOS keeps keychain items after an app is deleted (the token still never leaves the device) — but the app never reuses it: on the first launch after a reinstall it deletes the token, and you have to connect to Drive again. Uninstalling alone does not revoke the permission in Google, so before uninstalling tap “Disconnect” or revoke access in your Google account settings;
  • temporary copies prepared for “Share” are deleted when the share sheet closes, when the project or document is deleted, and on the next app launch;
  • technical entries in the system log (section 4) are deleted automatically by iOS.

11. Security

App data is protected by iOS mechanisms: the app sandbox, device encryption and the passcode. The Google session refresh token is kept in the keychain, on this device only. Connections to Google are encrypted (HTTPS). We recommend enabling a device passcode — it is the most effective protection for data stored locally.

12. Legal basis and transfers outside the EEA

Where data protection law (in particular the GDPR) applies, we process only the data that reaches us (section 2), based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — our legitimate interest in:

  • answering the messages you send us and resolving the matters you raise;
  • diagnosing and fixing bugs in the app using bug reports, and crash reports that Apple passes to us only if you agree to it in iOS Settings (section 6) — you give that consent to Apple and can withdraw it there at any time.

You have the right to object to this processing at any time (section 13). We do not process the data the app handles locally on your device — iOS system permissions (camera, location, microphone, speech recognition) are a technical safeguard of the device, not a basis for any processing by us. Data on your Drive is processed by Google under your agreement with Google.

We never receive your documents, photos, location or notes. The only data that reaches us is e-mail you choose to send (sections 8 and 14), crash reports from Apple — with your consent — and aggregated statistics from Apple, which are not personal data (section 6); we do not pass any of it on to anyone. Apple (iCloud Mail, App Store), Google and GitHub (website hosting) may process data on servers outside the European Economic Area (including the USA) under their own mechanisms, such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses — see their policies for details.

13. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection, as well as the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (in Poland: the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, UODO).

Because the data exists only on your device and your Google Drive, you exercise these rights directly: by viewing, exporting, renaming or deleting documents and projects — in the app, in the Files app or on Google Drive. For messages you have sent us, or if you need help, write to us — we will reply promptly and no later than within one month.

14. Website and e-mail correspondence

  • Website — The app’s website (description, support and this policy) uses no cookies, analytics, ads or third-party resources and stores nothing you type on it. It is served by GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. (USA), which — like any web server — may record IP addresses and technical browser data in its logs for security purposes. Details: docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement.
  • Contact form — The form on the support page sends nothing by itself: it opens your mail app with a ready message. Before anything reaches us, you see it and tap “Send” yourself.
  • E-mail — When you write to pdfpinapp@icloud.com — from the website, from the app or directly — we receive your e-mail address, the sender name set in your mail app, the message and any attachments. We use them only to reply and resolve the matter you raised. The mailbox runs on Apple’s iCloud Mail, which stores messages on Apple’s servers.
  • How long — We delete correspondence once the matter is closed and the message is no longer needed — no later than 12 months after the matter is closed — or promptly at your request.

15. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app processes data (for example by adding a new cloud service), we will update this policy together with a new app version and raise the version number and date in the header. The current text is always available in the app (Settings → Privacy → Privacy policy) and on the app’s website.
